\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries Probabilistic Coherence as a Test of Clinical LLM Reasoning}
\end{center}
\vspace{-2pt}

\subsection*{Abstract}
\footnotesize
Bayesian belief revision is order invariant as a theorem, since $P(d \mid e_1 \dots e_n)$ is a
function of the evidence set and not of the sequence in which it arrives. This gives a correctness
criterion for clinical language models that needs no annotator, no judge model and no independence
assumption, because the target value is known to be zero exactly. We evaluate eleven open weight
models from 4B to 32B parameters, spanning general, medically tuned and reasoning distilled
families, on 1.12 million elicited responses over 1{,}956 DDXPlus patients and 49 pathologies.
Every divergence is reported against three anchors measured on the same model, namely its test
retest floor, its between patient ceiling, and a corpus level generator noise floor of 0.0490
established from exact replicate cases in DDXPlus itself. Three findings follow. The anchors
reverse the direction of the headline result, since raw permutation divergence rises with model
scale while the anchored order effect does not. Belief updates do not track the evidence, with
direction agreement against an assumption free empirical oracle spanning 0.454 to 0.549 across all
eleven models. Most consequentially, every model names the correct diagnosis two to five times more
often than its own stated posterior ranks it first, and agrees with the top entry of its own
distribution on only 3 to 12 percent of items. The deficit is therefore in the probabilistic
interface rather than in the clinical knowledge, which motivates ELR Fusion, a symbolic combination
layer that is order invariant by construction, exactly and at zero marginal query cost, in all 132
configurations tested. We report the baseline that bounds that claim. Presenting every case in one
fixed canonical order reaches the same exact zero at an accuracy cost between $-0.012$ and $+0.021$
top 1, so a zero order effect is obtainable for free and is not on its own a contribution. What the
symbolic layer adds is invariance to which order is chosen, since posteriors under two different
fixed orders sit 0.120 to 0.258 divergence apart, together with a per finding audit trail.

\section{Methodology}


\subsection{Coherence axioms}
The instrument tests four properties of belief revision, each with an exact target and none
requiring human annotation. A1 and A4 are label free; A2 and A3 need ground truth, and the choice
of reference is the main methodological commitment of the study.

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}llc@{}}
\toprule
Axiom & Property & Target \\
\midrule
A1 & Order invariance, $P(d \mid e_1 \dots e_n)$ depends on the evidence set and not its order & 0 \\
A2 & Update fidelity, adding a finding moves belief as the corpus says it should & slope 1 \\
A3 & Redundancy insensitivity, certified uninformative evidence must not move belief & 0 \\
A4 & Positional anchoring, no position in the sequence carries special weight & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Data, models and the absent knowledge base}
The corpus is DDXPlus [1], comprising 1{,}292{,}579 synthetic patients over 49
pathologies and 223 findings. A data audit established two facts that constrain the design. The
released knowledge base declares 888 pathology by evidence slots and supplies conditional
probabilities for none of them, so exact posteriors for arbitrary evidence subsets cannot be read
off it. Reconstructing those tables from corpus frequencies and recombining them under conditional
independence reproduces the shipped differentials at mean Jensen Shannon divergence 0.546, roughly
eleven times the generator noise floor, and was therefore rejected. The posterior is instead
conditioned directly on all 1{,}292{,}579 released patients with Wilson intervals and a minimum
support of 500 cases, which makes A2 and A3 absolute while making no independence assumption.

The audit also found the ground truth differential to be stochastic. Across 21{,}004 replicate
pairs, that is patients identical in evidence multiset, age, sex, initial evidence and true
pathology, the shipped differential differs for 25.8 percent of pairs at mean divergence 0.0490.
Every DDXPlus differential matching metric therefore has a ceiling below 1.0, which applies to any
work on this corpus. Eleven models were evaluated: Qwen3 at 4B, 8B and 32B in thinking and
nonthinking configurations, MedGemma 4B, MedGemma 27B, Med42 8B, R1 Distill 32B and GPT OSS 20B.

\subsection{Three anchors and a competence gate}
A raw divergence is uninterpretable alone, since a model that resamples noisily appears incoherent
regardless of ordering. Each model is measured against three references computed on that same model
and the same items: a test retest floor from resampling the identical prompt, a between patient
ceiling from unrelated patients, and the corpus generator floor of 0.0490. The anchored order
effect is the gap from the floor as a fraction of the floor to ceiling distance.

A competence gate precedes all coherence reporting, because schema validity constrains the shape of
a response and not its content, and a model returning a constant scores a near zero order effect
that reads as excellent coherence. During calibration GPT OSS 20B under a JSON grammar returned an
identical minimal object for essentially every case at 1.000 schema validity and 1.000 informative
rate, scoring 0.039 top 1 accuracy against a chance rate of 0.020. The cause is the harmony response
format, which requires a channel token at position zero that a JSON grammar forbids. Decoded freely
and parsed from the final channel, the same model on the same items reaches 0.653, a change of 16.6
times from one decoding flag. All reported results use the corrected path.

\subsection{ELR Fusion}
Log odds accumulate over an evidence set, so the result is order invariant by construction:
$\operatorname{logodds}(d \mid E) = \operatorname{logodds}(d) + \tau \sum_{i} \log \mathrm{LR}(e_i \mid d)$.
The likelihood ratio table is elicited once per finding for the whole corpus, so a new case costs
zero additional model queries. Because the sum ranges over an unordered set, and scaling an order
invariant quantity leaves it order invariant, the guarantee holds for every value of $\tau$. That
coefficient is the log odds form of temperature scaling [6] applied to the evidence term so
the prior is untouched, correcting the overcounting that arises because correlated findings are
summed as though independent. It is fitted by cross fitting over two folds, so the coefficient
applied to a case is never fitted on that case.

\subsection{A1, the anchors reverse the finding}
Permutation divergence rises with scale across the Qwen3 ladder, from 0.128 at 4B to 0.291 at 32B,
which read alone would indicate that larger models are less coherent. The test retest floor rises
faster over the same ladder, from 0.072 to 0.256, so the anchored effect falls from 0.215 to 0.127.
Reporting the raw number alone inverts the conclusion, which is the strongest argument for the three
anchor design and the reason prior order effect figures are not directly comparable to these.

The effect is real but not universal. Eight of eleven models show an order effect whose bootstrap
interval excludes zero. For MedGemma 4B, Med42 8B and GPT OSS 20B the interval covers zero, and in
each case the floor has risen close to the ceiling, which is a collapsing denominator rather than a
coherence success. The top 1 flip rate is high throughout, from 0.699 to 0.994, so reordering the
same findings changes the leading diagnosis for most patients even where distributional movement is
modest.

\subsection{A2, A3 and A4}
\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Axiom & Quantity & Range over eleven models & Ideal \\
\midrule
A2 & Slope $\beta$ against the empirical oracle & 0.0009 to 0.0576 & 1.0 \\
A2 & Coefficient of determination $r^2$ & at most 0.0207 & 1.0 \\
A2 & Direction agreement & 0.454 to 0.549 & 1.0 \\
A3 & Mean divergence from uninformative evidence & 0.0759 to 0.2687 & 0 \\
A3 & Cases moved beyond the model's own floor & 15.8 to 44.9 percent & 0 \\
A3 & Ninetieth percentile of that divergence & up to 0.928 & 0 \\
A4 & Models with a significant positional slope & 7 of 11 & 0 of 11 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A2 is the strongest negative result. Whether a model's belief in a diagnosis rises or falls when a
finding is added is uncorrelated with whether the corpus says it should, and direction agreement
straddles chance for every model. Six of eleven have a slope interval excluding zero, so the effect
is real, but at $\beta$ below 0.058 against an ideal of 1.0 it is swamped. Unlike A1 this is a
failure of direction measured against ground truth, with no independence assumption available to
attack.

A3 requires care. Evidence the corpus certifies as uninformative moves the posterior beyond the
model's own resampling noise for 15.8 to 44.9 percent of cases and flips the leading diagnosis for
26.5 to 69.8 percent. The mean spurious divergence, however, sits below the model's own retest floor
for ten of eleven models. The distribution is heavy tailed rather than uniformly shifted, so the
correct claim is that irrelevant findings move belief substantially for a large minority of cases,
not that they do so on average.

For A4 the directional reading matters. A divergence based position measure is near zero throughout,
between $-0.004$ and $0.014$, but Jensen Shannon divergence is symmetric and cancels a consistent
directional shift. Regressing the target log odds on position instead, seven of eleven models show a
slope whose bootstrap interval excludes zero. Six are primacy, meaning the decisive finding carries
more weight when presented first, and they comprise every Qwen3 configuration together with R1
Distill 32B. MedGemma 27B alone reverses to recency. A symmetric divergence measure is blind to the
effect the regression detects, which is worth noting for anyone measuring position bias this way.

\subsection{The answer and belief gap}
Each model was asked, on the same item and with the same prompt content, both to name one diagnosis
and to score all 49 candidates for plausibility. The two tasks share an item identifier, so the
comparison is exactly paired. Every model is markedly more accurate when naming a diagnosis than
when ranking its own distribution. Self agreement below is the share of items on which the named
diagnosis equals the top entry of that model's own posterior.

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Model & Paired items & Named & Posterior & Ratio & Self agreement & Median rank \\
\midrule
Med42 8B        & 35{,}208 & 0.309 & 0.079 & 3.89 & 0.121 & 3 \\
Qwen3 8B        & 35{,}208 & 0.281 & 0.109 & 2.58 & 0.085 & 3 \\
Qwen3 8B think  & 35{,}208 & 0.252 & 0.097 & 2.58 & 0.067 & 3 \\
Qwen3 4B        & 35{,}205 & 0.267 & 0.059 & 4.51 & 0.042 & 4 \\
Qwen3 4B think  & 32{,}696 & 0.235 & 0.049 & 4.81 & 0.019 & 3 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A McNemar test on Med42 8B gives $\chi^2 = 7{,}131$ over 8{,}633 versus 543 discordant pairs. Three
confounds were excluded. Uniform posteriors cannot explain the effect, since Med42 8B emits a
uniform vector on 0.3 percent of items and shows the largest gap. Ties at the top cannot explain it,
since restricting to strictly unimodal posteriors leaves accuracy at 0.081 against 0.309 named.
Ordering cannot explain it, since the comparison is paired within item. The median rank of the
model's own named diagnosis inside its own distribution is 3 or 4 of 49, against a chance value of
25. The posterior therefore encodes the answer and declines to rank it first. This is not a
knowledge failure and not sampling noise. It is a failure to express what the model knows as a
coherent distribution, and it explains the A2 null, since a model whose probabilistic interface is
broken cannot move that interface in the direction the evidence requires.

\subsection{Remedies}
Paired over the cases every method produced, for Qwen3 32B:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrl@{}}
\toprule
Method & Order residual & Queries per case & Top 1 & Top 5 & Entropy & Exact \\
\midrule
Direct                  & 0.2988 & 1 & 0.1892 & 0.3006 & 3.94 & no \\
Self consistency        & 0.1244 & 6 & 0.2265 & 0.4346 & 4.50 & no \\
Permutation ensemble K5 & 0.1038 & 5 & 0.2377 & 0.4443 & 4.53 & approximate \\
ELR Fusion              & 0.0000 & 0 & 0.1334 & 0.2480 & 0.86 & yes \\
ELR Fusion calibrated   & 0.0000 & 0 & 0.0997 & 0.2393 & 5.25 & yes \\
Fixed canonical order   & 0.0000 & 1 & 0.1915 & ---    & ---  & yes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Permutation ensembling buys its residual with queries, decays only as $O(1/\sqrt{K})$, and no value
of $K$ reaches zero. ELR Fusion reaches zero exactly, and does so for all eleven models at all
twelve values of $\tau$, that is 132 of 132 configurations, because the weight table is elicited
once for the whole corpus rather than once per case.

A fixed canonical order reaches the same exact zero, and it is free. Presenting every case in one
deterministic order removes the order effect by construction, at an accuracy cost between $-0.012$
and $+0.021$ top 1 against a randomly chosen fixed order, and the canonical order is the better of
the two for nine of eleven models. ELR Fusion attains the identical zero and pays 5.6 top 1 points.
A zero order effect is therefore obtainable for free, and reporting one is not on its own a
contribution.

What a fixed order does not do is remove the dependence. It conceals it by freezing one arbitrary
choice. The separating quantity is not the residual but the spread across fixed orders: the
canonical order posterior sits 0.120 to 0.258 divergence from the posterior under a different fixed
order, a distance that is zero by construction for an order invariant method and is the price of the
arbitrary choice for a fixed one. That spread, together with the per finding audit trail, is what
the symbolic layer buys, and it is a narrower claim than the one an exact zero alone would support.

The honest cost is accuracy, and it is model dependent rather than fixed. Across the eleven models
ELR Fusion retains between 0.30 and 1.06 of direct elicitation accuracy, median 0.71, and for two
models it is more accurate than direct elicitation. The shrinkage coefficient is likewise a frontier
rather than a fitted constant, since the accuracy optimal $\tau$ reaches at least 2.0 for eight of eleven
models while the optimum for the likelihood of the truth falls at or below 0.10 for ten of eleven,
a difference of more than an order of magnitude in the same model. Order invariance is exact at every
point on that frontier.

\section{Conclusion}
Three claims are supported. First, order effects in clinical language models cannot be interpreted
without anchors measured on the same model, because the test retest floor rises with scale faster
than the effect does and the raw number therefore inverts the comparison. Second, the failure these
models exhibit is not primarily a reasoning failure. They identify the correct diagnosis at up to 32
times the chance rate while ranking that same diagnosis third or fourth of 49 in their own stated
distribution, and they agree with themselves on fewer than one item in eight. The deficit lies in the
probabilistic interface. Third, because the deficit is one of representation rather than knowledge,
moving the probability arithmetic into a symbolic layer is the structurally indicated fix, and ELR
Fusion attains an exactly zero order effect at zero marginal query cost in every configuration
tested, at an accuracy cost reported in full rather than concealed. That third claim is deliberately
narrow. A fixed canonical order reaches the same zero at no accuracy cost, so what the symbolic
layer contributes is invariance to which order is chosen and auditability per finding, not the zero
itself.

Three limitations are stated plainly. DDXPlus is synthetic, which is what makes an exact reference
possible for A2 and A3, but an external validity arm on real clinical narratives is required before
these magnitudes are claimed for deployed settings. The elicited likelihood ratios, rather than the
fusion rule, set the accuracy ceiling of the method, and improving that elicitation is where the
remaining accuracy lies. Zero marginal query cost is marginal only: the likelihood ratio table costs
497 elicitation queries per model to build, which amortises over a corpus but not over a small
deployment, and it assumes a closed finding vocabulary.
