HEADLINE: FOUR RESULTS THAT CHANGE THE PAPER'S CLAIMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before the itemised replies, four findings that the review's questions
produced and that require the paper's framing to change. We would rather
state them plainly than have them found at review.

(a) A FIXED CANONICAL ORDER IS FREE (comment 1). Presenting every case in one
    deterministic order gives exactly zero order effect at essentially zero
    accuracy cost (-0.012 to +0.021 top-1 vs a random fixed order; canonical
    is slightly BETTER for 9 of 11 models). ELR-Fusion attains the same zero
    and pays 5.6 top-1 points. The "exact zero order effect" contribution as
    written is not a contribution.

(b) THE FUSION RULE WAS NEVER THE PROBLEM (comment 2). Replacing the
    model-elicited log-LR table with one estimated from the 1.29M-patient
    corpus, changing nothing else in the recombination, moves top-1 from
    0.133 to 0.9765 (top-5 1.000). The accuracy cost attributed to
    ELR-Fusion belongs entirely to the elicited weights.

(c) THE A1 TOP-1 FLIP RATE WAS MOSTLY NOISE (comment 21). The retest flip
    rate - the leading diagnosis changing when nothing changes at all - is
    0.53 to 0.95. The reported permutation flip rate of 0.70 to 0.99 exceeds
    it by only 0.04 to 0.27.

(d) THE ORDER EFFECT IS SMALL, AND ABSENT FOR THREE MODELS (comment 18).
    Absolute gaps are 0.002 to 0.056 JSD. For MedGemma-1.5-4B, Med42-8B and
    GPT-OSS-20B the patient-clustered 95% CI includes zero.

1. CANONICAL-ORDERING BASELINE
   ACCEPTED - MEASURED. The comment is correct and the effect is larger than
   suggested. The battery already contained a canonical arm (the DDXPlus
   released code-sorted order, 2 replicates/case), so this is measured, not
   argued.  [reports/tables/rev_canonical_baseline.csv]

     model              canonical  random-fixed   cost
     GPT-OSS-20B          0.4055     0.3930      +0.0125
     Qwen3-32B            0.1915     0.1845      +0.0070
     MedGemma-27B         0.1127     0.1015      +0.0112
     R1-Distill-32B       0.0987     0.1110      -0.0124
     (11 models; cost range -0.012 to +0.021)

   A fixed order therefore dominates ELR-Fusion on both axes reported in
   Table 1: same exact zero, no accuracy cost.

   What a fixed order does NOT do is remove the dependence - it conceals it
   by fixing one arbitrary choice. The canonical-order posterior sits 0.120
   to 0.258 JSD away from the posterior under a randomly chosen fixed order
   (same table, column canonical_vs_random_jsd). That distance is zero by
   construction for an order-invariant method and is the price of the
   arbitrary choice for a fixed order.

   PAPER CHANGE: the canonical baseline is added to Table 1 as a first-class
   row. The ELR-Fusion claim is narrowed from "achieves exact zero order
   effect" to "is invariant to WHICH fixed order is chosen, and is auditable
   per finding" - and we now state explicitly that zero order effect alone is
   obtainable for free.

2. ELR-FUSION IS A CONDITIONAL-INDEPENDENCE RECOMBINATION
   ACCEPTED - MEASURED. The comment identifies a genuine internal tension,
   and the ablation settles which half of it matters.
   [coherence/analysis/corpus_lr_ablation.py -> rev_corpus_lr_ablation.csv]

   Same log-linear rule, same evidence sets, same 1,956 patients; only the
   log-LR table changes:

     weights source                 best top-1   top-5    NLL
     corpus-derived (1.29M cases)     0.9765     1.000    0.037
     elicited: GPT-OSS-20B            0.2592     0.618    3.378
     elicited: Qwen3-32B-think        0.1498     0.273    3.730
     elicited: Qwen3-32B              0.0946     0.237    3.858
     elicited: MedGemma-1.5-4B        0.0194     0.099    4.190

   So the recombination rule is not the limitation; the elicited weights are.
   Elicited tables reach 2% to 27% of what the same rule reaches with
   corpus-estimated weights.

   On the apparent contradiction with the naive-Bayes rejection: these are
   two different targets and both results stand. Naive-Bayes recombination
   fails to reproduce the DDXPlus DIFFERENTIAL DISTRIBUTION (JSD 0.546 vs the
   0.0490 floor) while being an excellent TOP-1 CLASSIFIER on the same data
   (0.9765). The paper conflated "cannot reconstruct the shipped
   differential" with "is not a usable recombination rule". That is corrected.

   On tau <= 0.10 for ten of eleven models: yes, this is itself a knowledge
   failure, and we now say so. A likelihood-optimal shrinkage that near zero
   means the best calibrated use of the elicited evidence term is to
   discount it almost entirely - i.e. the elicited log-LRs carry little
   reliable information. This is now presented as a FINDING about elicited
   likelihood ratios, not as a tuning detail.

     named diagnosis              beats it for  11 / 11 models

     logprob, summed              beats it for   6 / 11 models

   What this does and does not support.

   SUPPORTED: the deficit is specific to the verbalized distribution. Asking
   the same weights for a NAME outperforms asking them for a 49-way
   distribution on every model without exception, by 0.058 to 0.427 top-1.
   That is the robust form of the "expression, not knowledge" claim and it no
   longer rests on the logprob path at all.

   PARTIALLY SUPPORTED: token scoring also beats the stated posterior, but
   only under length-normalisation (10/11), not under summed log-probability
   (6/11). We report both rather than the flattering one.

   NOT SUPPORTED - one clear counterexample: GPT-OSS-20B. Its stated posterior
   (0.3563) beats both logprob variants (0.1304 summed, 0.0680 normalised).
   It is also the one model decoded free-form rather than grammar-constrained
   (comment 7, C11), so the elicitation path that was broken before the fix is
   now its strongest. We state this rather than exclude it.

   A caveat on the summed rule, which the data exposes. Summed log-probability
   favours SHORT diagnosis names, so for several models one short label wins
   top-1 almost always while the true label still ranks top-5. The signature
   is a large top5-minus-top1 gap: R1-Distill-32B 0.619, Med42-8B 0.544,
   MedGemma-27B 0.510, against 0.089 for GPT-OSS-20B. Length-normalisation
   removes that bias (Med42-8B 0.0204 -> 0.1237) but discards the genuine
   prior that longer names are less likely. Neither rule is uniquely correct,
   which is why both are in the table.

   The earlier 120-case pilot (Qwen3-8B, 2.1x) overstated the effect. The
   full-sweep figure for that model is 1.38x under length-normalisation. The
   pilot number is withdrawn.

7. DECODING REGIME FOR NAMING VS SCORING
   ACCEPTED - STATED. Both tasks use identical decoding within a model:
   grammar-constrained (xgrammar, disable_any_whitespace=True) for 10 of 11
   models, and free decoding with final-channel parsing for GPT-OSS-20B only,
   for the reason in comment 7's own reference (harmony forbids a JSON token
   at position 0; see C11). So naming and scoring are NOT confounded by
   decoding within any model. The GPT-OSS deviation is per-model, applies to
   both tasks equally, and is now stated in Methods rather than only in the
   calibration appendix.

8. ARE THE 49 SCORES A DISTRIBUTION? IS LIST ORDER FIXED?
   ACCEPTED - STATED, and one confound conceded.
   - The scores are INDEPENDENT ratings. The prompt says "Score each
     candidate diagnosis from 0 to 100 ... The scores do NOT need to sum to
     anything." They are normalised to a simplex afterwards for the
     divergence computation. This was a deliberate calibration decision (C6):
     asking for probabilities summing to 1 produced 62.9% uniform collapse.
     The paper will now say "normalised plausibility ratings", not
     "posterior", wherever the elicited object is meant.
   - The candidate list order is FIXED (alphabetical) in every prompt, for
     every model and every item. So list order cannot confound any
     WITHIN-item comparison (A1, A3, A4 are all paired within item). It does
     leave a constant positional bias that could inflate or depress
     particular pathologies uniformly; we now say so, and the token-logprob
     baseline (comment 6) omits the list entirely and so is free of it.

9. "NOT A KNOWLEDGE FAILURE" OVERREACHES
   ACCEPTED - MEASURED, and the claim is withdrawn as written. The upper
   bound the comment asks for is now measured: the same log-linear rule with
   corpus-estimated weights reaches top-1 0.9765 / top-5 1.000 on these
   1,956 patients (comment 2). Against that bound:

     corpus-derived log-linear      0.9765   (100% of bound)
     GPT-OSS-20B, named             0.6510   ( 67%)

     Qwen3-32B, stated posterior    0.1691   ( 17%)
     MedGemma-1.5-4B, named         0.0805   (  8%)

   A model that reaches 55-67% of a bound attainable by counting
   co-occurrences in the training corpus has a knowledge gap as well as an
   expression gap. The paper will claim only the narrower, supported point:
   the expression gap is REAL AND SEPARABLE, not that knowledge is intact.

   The separability now rests on the NAMED path, which beats the stated
   posterior for 11 of 11 models (comment 6). The token-logprob path also
   beats it, but for 10 of 11 under length-normalisation and only 6 of 11
   under summed scoring, with GPT-OSS-20B a clear counterexample under both.
   An earlier draft of this reply asserted that both paths beat the stated
   posterior; that was written from a 120-case pilot and the full sweep does
   not support it for logprobs. Corrected here.

   On "32x chance": qualified. It is driven by GPT-OSS-20B (0.653); the
   median model is 13.8x, and MedGemma-1.5-4B is 3.9x. Chance (1/49) is also
   a weak reference on a class-balanced 49-way task; the corpus-derived bound
   is the reference we now use.

14. HOW IS EVIDENCE "CERTIFIED UNINFORMATIVE"?
    ACCEPTED - STATED, with the limitation conceded. Definition: a finding is
    certified uninformative for a context when adding it to the conditioning
    set moves the empirical posterior by JSD < 1e-3 while retaining >= 500
    patients of support. The comment is right that a Wilson interval at
    n = 500 resolves only effects of a few percentage points, and that a
    finding can be irrelevant to common pathologies while mattering to rare
    ones. We now (i) report the support actually retained per distractor,
    (ii) state the detectable-effect floor explicitly, and (iii) flag that
    certification is with respect to the full 49-way distribution, so
    rare-pathology effects below the resolution are not excluded.

15. ORACLE CONDITIONS ON E SUPERSET-OF S
